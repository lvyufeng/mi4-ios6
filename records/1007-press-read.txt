## VERDICT — the frontier is a FAULT STORM, and 1007 makes it visible

**The abort path is where the ~24.4 s freeze lives, and the "~40 aborts" the run has been read as
since 1004 is a RECORD-CAP ARTIFACT, not the abort count.**

The 1007 witness brackets every `sleh_abort` with the free-running generic timer and publishes its
records through `entry_write_kv` — the **direct, cap-exempt** channel (the one `entry_tick_summary`
uses, `entry_stubs.c:1909-1912`). The 1004-era `xnu_live_sleh_*` keys go through the **capped** live
channel (`ENTRY_LIVE_CAP 24576`, `:2403`). This run's live channel filled and published its cap marker:

```
xnu_live_cap=0x00006000 (24576)  @:3933        (the header)
xnu_live_capped=0x00006000 (24576) @:29127     (the channel stopped appending here)
```

The last `xnu_live_sleh_seen` this log carries is **40** (@:28159) — 968 lines *before* the cap. The
1007 ladder, read from the direct channel, tells the real story:

| abort ordinal | gap (s) | handler call (s) | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 … 4 | 0.00 – 0.69 | ~0 | dyld walking its image (1004's set) |

`xnu_live_abt_n == xnu_live_abt_seq` at **every** one of the 23 published rows (cross-checked), and the
disassembly confirms `entry_note_abt_span` reads `g_sleh_seq` directly (`801...= [r4,#120]`,
`r4=0x8060f000`; `g_sleh_seq` at `0x8060f078`). So `abt_seq` **is** `g_sleh_seq` and both reach
**2,097,152**: the run took **~2.1 million aborts**, not 40. The live channel hid every one past #40
behind its cap; the direct channel did not. (0x200000 is the *last published* ladder row, so the count
is `>= 2^21 = 2,097,152` and `< 2^22 = 4,194,304` — the run would have published a `0x400000` row
had it reached one. The magnitude is not in doubt; only the exact ordinal is bracketed.)

**This corrects 1004's central claim.** 1004 read `sleh_seq×40` and concluded "40 aborts, a WALK
through dyld's image". The 40 is where the **capped live channel** stopped, not where the aborts did.
The first ~40 aborts *are* the dyld walk (their `sleh_far`s step through `0x2be…`, the dyld image);
what follows is a **storm** the capped channel never showed.

**Why the *sleh* records stop at 40 — the channel was nearly full, and the fault handler's page-ins
filled the rest.** The run image's band is 512 (`ABORT_TRACE=1`); the disassembly of `entry_note_sleh`
shows it writing the per-entry frame band for **every** `seq ≤ 512` (`cmp r1, #512` / `bls`), so every
abort up to #512 must have written a `sleh_seen` record *if the channel accepted it*. It did not. The
channel's record counter is measurable: the last `sleh_seen` (#40) lands when the live channel holds
**24,023 of its 24,576 records — 97.8 % full** — and the 968 live records that follow it are the SDHCI
storage transport (`xnu_live_storage_dr_*`, `xnu_live_storage_rd_*`, ~10 per driver read, LBA
`0x400000→0x4f4987`, a page-in) that the fault handler performs: aborts **41…512 would have written,
but by the time they ran the channel was full** and `entry_live_write` refused them
(`g_live_records >= ENTRY_LIVE_CAP`). So this is a **channel-FULL** artifact, not a channel-*down* one
(`g_live_state` can only become 2 inside `entry_live_init`'s install checks, `entry_stubs.c:2248-2554`;
it does not flip mid-run), and the marker `xnu_live_capped=0x00006000` is the channel reaching its cap,
not a refusal. That the aborts continued past #40 is independently visible in the **direct** channel:
`abt_n=0x40` (abort #64) appears *after* the cap marker, and the ladder runs to `0x200000`.

**1004's own checker has the same blind spot, and its *own* capture proves it.** `check_1004_capture.py`
reads the cap *positionally* — if `xnu_live_capped` lands after the last `sleh` record it prints "the
abort trace is COMPLETE" (`:716`). In 1004's own capture
(`recovered-unknown-arm-20261013-160126-last_kmsg.txt`) the last `sleh` record is at :28020 and the cap
at :29006 — *after* it — so the checker called the trace complete while the storm ran on invisibly.
The positional test is right for what it measures (no `sleh` record was dropped *before* the cap) but
wrong for the verdict it draws: **a cap after the last *published* `sleh` record does not mean the
aborts stopped** — it means the channel did. The `_storm` counter that was meant to say otherwise
(`xnu_live_sleh_storm`, `entry_stubs.c:1814`) rides the **same capped channel** and so can never fire
once the channel is full. The only instrument that saw the true count is the direct-channel one 1007
added (`xnu_live_abt_n`).

## The freeze magnitude, exactly

`xnu_live_tick_sum_maxd` is a **running maximum** republished 14×. Its largest (and last) value is
`0x1be892af` = **468,226,735 counts = 24.387 s** at 19.2 MHz — the 1004/1005/1006 reading reproduced
(a **10,578-count** / 551 µs drift from 1005's `0x1be8bc01`, i.e. the same freeze, not a new one).

The abort pointer sits **just under** it:

```
xnu_live_abt_maxcall = 0x1570e6a5 = 359,720,613 counts = 18.735 s   (0.768 × M)
xnu_live_abt_maxgap  = 0x1570e6e9 = 359,720,681 counts = 18.735 s   (0.768 × M)
```

**The load-bearing number is `maxcall` alone.** The recorder brackets every `sleh_abort` with the
free-running timer (`entry_note_abt_span(t0, entry_counter()-t0)`), so `maxcall` = the longest *single*
`sleh_abort` invocation the run took = **18.735 s**. Its ladder (the `xnu_live_abt_call` records, not
just the latch) corroborates it independently: the 0x100 row shows a 5.322 s handler, 0x200 shows
3.776 s, and the latch climbs monotonically (`0x631 → … → 0x1570e6a5`). So **the ~18.7 s is inside one
abort**, measured directly.

**`maxgap` adds nothing and must not be presented as a second witness.** The recorder defines
`gap(k) = t0(k) − t0(k−1) = call(k−1) + outside(k−1)`, where `outside` is the CPU time *between* abort
*k−1*'s return and abort *k*'s entry. This is an **identity**: `maxgap ≥ maxcall` always, and the two
maxima are the *same* invocation seen once as `call` and once as the next `gap` — the 68-count
(3.54 µs) difference is exactly `outside(maxcall-invocation)`. So the pairing is a **tautology, not
evidence** for (C); it cannot *by construction* distinguish (C) from (B). The (B) reading — "the freeze
is *between* two aborts" — is ruled out instead by what `maxcall` measures: 18.7 s was spent with the
wrapper's second `entry_counter()` not yet reached, i.e. *inside* the call, not after it.

The classifier's (C) verdict is therefore carried by `maxcall` (18.7 s < M = 24.39 s: **77 % of the
freeze is inside one abort invocation**), with the remaining **5.65 s** (`M − maxcall`) not further
localised by this instrument:

> **(C) ABORT-PATH LOCALISED — INSIDE ONE ABORT:** a single `sleh_abort` (its `arm_fast_fault` +
> `vm_fault` body) took ~18.7 s. The frontier is **inside one fault**; a further ~5.65 s of the 24.39 s
> freeze is not attributed by 1007's two clocks.

## The stall frame (resolved by value)

`xnu_live_stall_pc=0x8001b5cd` = **`enable_interrupts+0x5`**; `xnu_live_stall_lr=0x8046c95c` =
**`sleh_abort+0x468`**; `xnu_live_stall_cpsr=0x60000113` (IRQ **masked**, `i=1`). The 1004 read pinned
this frame on "the sleh return path / `ml_cause_interrupt`". The 1007 data relocates it: the frame is
the CPU **slowly making its way through fault handling with interrupts masked**, inside the
storm — `sleh_abort` at `+0x468` returning toward `enable_interrupts`, the `cpsie i`'s own `push`.
The `cpsr=0x60000113` `i=1` (IRQ masked) is *consistent* with a storm that keeps re-entering the
exception by a **synchronous** fault (a data abort masks what the current CPSR masks; it does not
re-enable anything), exactly what the flat `gap≈0, call≈0` rows show between the two 18.7 s ones.

## What this does and does NOT say

- **DOES say:** the frontier is the abort path — specifically **a single `sleh_abort` invocation of
  ~18.7 s**, riding a **fault storm of ~2.1 million aborts** that the capped channel hid. 1006's
  "non-idle" reading now has a location: the storm is the non-idle work.
- **DOES say (correction):** 1004's "40 aborts, a WALK" is a **cap artifact** — the count is
  ~2,097,152. The dyld-walk reading held only for the first ~40; the run then enters a storm.
- **DOES NOT say:** the fault *address* of the storm. The storm rows are past the live channel's cap,
  so `sleh_far` stops at #40 (`0x3ef2f000`, a kernel copyin — `recover=0x8049652c`, `user=0`). The
  `sleh_far` for the 18.7 s abort is **not in this log** — that reading needs the `far` published on
  the direct channel (1008's task).
- **No brick:** the machine is alive (`No errors detected`); the run ended in a resident non-return as
  expected for this arm.
