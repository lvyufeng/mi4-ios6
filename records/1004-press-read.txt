## The frontier: NOT abort #40 — the machine survives it and then the clock decays

Abort #40 is the kernel's first touch of the shared-cache slide page (`far=0x3e8dd000`) **inside a
`copyin`** (pc 0x80495590 `.Lcopyin_guts`), and the machine **does not crash on it** (no panic,
death gate OPEN, `sleh_storm` absent — no abort #41). **It survives and keeps going**: storage
reads keep completing (`served` 0x39→0x3e, LBA `0x4a3810→0x4a3830`), and pid 1's thread `0xc0bfa000`
makes FURTHER `mach_absolute_time` syscalls (`xnu_live_usvc_num=0xfffffffd` == `_mach_absolute_time`:
the commpage-timebase-invalid fallback, `svc #128`) at `lr` **advancing** `0x2bf08a9f→0x2bf08af1→
0x2bf08b11`. dyld is inside **`ImageLoader::link`**, snapshotting absolute time — and its return
address MOVES, so it is truly executing, not re-trapped.

The run's real end-state is the instrument's own **tick summary**: `xnu_live_tick_sum_maxd` reaches
`0x1be8b709` ≈ **24.4 s** (the max inter-tick gap; 468,236,041 ticks / 19.2 MHz) while `tick_sum_count` drags and `wdt_pets` climbs
to 0x61. The machine is alive (ticks, pets, storage) but tick DELIVERY **collapses** — the STALL
999/1003 named. The frozen sampler frame (below) is the last thing the *tick* saw, held while the
synchronous paths continued.

The frame held at every late tick sample (n=9..15, pc=0x8001b1fd, `cpsr=0x60000113` SVC mode,
`sp=0xcfdbbf00`, `lr=0x8046b92c`) is **one frame, captured once and then STALE** — not a dwell.
`lr=0x8046b92c` is `sleh_abort+0x468` (right after `bl ml_set_interrupts_enabled`), and the pc
falls in `enable_interrupts` (`ml_set_interrupts_enabled`(1) falls through into `astloop`/
`enable_interrupts`: 0x8001b1ac AST check → `bl ast_taken`; 0x8001b1f8 `cpsie i`; 0x8001b1fc
`push {r0,r1,r2,r7,r8,lr}`). `pc=0x8001b1fd` is **byte 2 of that `push`** — a transient pass-through
in the prologue, NOT a spin word (`astloop`'s only backward branch is `b 0x8001b1ac` at 0x8001b1f4;
nothing branches to 0x8001b1fd). One EARLIER sample (n=8, @:27345) is USER mode (`cpsr=0x40000010`)
at dyld `0x2be1aecc` = `_open`+0x8 (`svc #5`) — proof the sampler reads real user frames and pid 1
RAN in user mode. **The same pc/`sp` appears on three DIFFERENT kernel stacks across the run**
(`0xc9d8bf58`/`0xc9d7bfb0` early, `0xcfdbbf00` late) — `enable_interrupts` is a high-frequency pc
every `ml_set_interrupts_enabled(1)` passes through, so seeing it is expected and says nothing about
a hang.

**CAUTION — the frozen frame may be a MEASUREMENT artifact, not the machine's true state.** Three
facts: (i) `pc=0x8001b1fd` is ODD, while every other sampled pc is even (Thumb/ARM interworking
clears bit 0); (ii) it sits two bytes into a `push`, a pc nothing branches to; (iii) the sample's
`_nz` (frame occupancy) changes between n=10 and n=11 (0x0e→0x0f) — **the channel is still being
written while pc/sp/lr/cpsr are held**, so the six identical samples are one held frame, not six
independent confirmations. `enable_interrupts` is a HIGH-FREQUENCY pc (every `ml_set_interrupts_
enabled(1)` falls through it, and the 999/1004 hook `entry_timer_edge_probe` now sits inside it at
0x8001b208), so a sampler that catches the CPU there repeatedly may be reading a common pc, not a
dwell. **This pass SETTLES it: the frame is the last thing the TICK saw, not the machine's state.**
The cap-exempt channels show the machine executing (usvc, storage) after this frame, and
`tick_sum_maxd`=0x1be8b709 says tick delivery collapsed — so the sampler stopped updating because
its own cadence collapsed, not because the CPU halted here. The `far=0x3e8dd000` page is never
re-recorded as a fault, consistent with it having been handled.

## Reading (honest scope)

**1004 REFUTES the 1003 candidate (a) "the same page re-faults".** The abort set is a clean
forward walk — zero repeats at both word and page granularity — over dyld's own ~24 pages, then a
handful of kernel-mode faults. **So 1005 branch (a) (CS-validation LOOP,
`STAGE90_XNU_CS_TAINT_TRACE`) is NOT the branch the evidence selects.**

**What is proven:**
1. pid 1 executed dyld's USER code (`ImageLoader::link` linking images) and faulted in its OWN
   pages forward, one page at a time — 34 of the 40 aborts are user-mode (`user=1`), dyld's image.
2. The other **6 aborts are KERNEL-mode** (`user=0`): seq 1-4, 37, 39, 40. Their `pc` does NOT
   resolve to `sleh_abort` — by value it lands in the copy primitives: seq 1/2/39/40 in
   `.Lcopyin_guts`/`copyinstr` (`bcopyinout.o`; pc 0x804959f0 / 0x80495e1c / 0x80495e10 /
   0x80495590), seq 3/37 in `bcopy`'s `L64loop` (0x80016440 / 0x8001644c). So these are the
   **kernel COPYING to/from an unmapped user address while servicing dyld** (strings, argv, a
   shared-cache header), not dyld faulting on its own code. Abort #40 — `far=0x3e8dd000` = the
   shared-cache slide page, pc 0x80495590 `.Lcopyin_guts`, `recover=0x804954fc` — is the kernel
   faulting **inside a `copyin`** whose source is the process's address space; the `copyin` step
   touches the shared-cache page. The `recover` word is non-zero ⇒ the handler had a plan for it
   (page in and retry, or convert to `EFAULT`).
3. **The 1003/1004 "abort #40 never returned" reading is RETRACTED — it is not supported by the
   evidence.** The missing `sleh_back` is REAL (39 backs for 40 seqs — verified), but it does NOT
   mean a lost return: **independent, cap-exempt evidence shows the machine keeps executing past
   @:28020** — pid 1's thread `0xc0bfa000` makes FURTHER `mach_absolute_time` syscalls
   (`xnu_live_usvc_num=0xfffffffd`, @29112/@29120, `lr` ADVANCING `0x2bf08a9f→0x2bf08af1→0x2bf08b11`)
   and the root-media keeps serving reads (`served` 0x39→0x3e, LBA `0x4a3810→0x4a3830`, `err` 0).
   The `sleh` entry note for #40 DID complete (`xnu_live_slot_ab_calls=0x28`, its last statement);
   only the after-`__real_sleh_abort` back-note is missing. **The first touch of the shared cache is
   NOT fatal**; dyld proceeds past it.
   - **A first-draft cause — a cross-CPU lost update on the non-atomic `g_sleh_seq`/`g_sleh_back` —
     is REFUTED**: the box boots UNIPROCESSOR (the capture's own `real_ncpus: 1`,
     `pmap_asid_ncpus: 1`; the MI4 header's `cpu_count=4` is the DEVICE-TREE value, not the booted
     count — [[mi4-one-value-two-definitions]]). With one CPU and every abort on the single thread
     `0xc0bfa000`, no such race can operate.
   - **Why the back-note is missing is NOT PROVEN either way.** It is not the channel cap (@:29006
     is ~24000 records after @:28020, and both the seq and back keys share the general channel), and
     not a race (uniprocessor). What is excluded is every *fatal* explanation; the back-note is a
     lost RECORD, not a lost return.
4. **Item 3's "no more syscalls" was a capped-channel artifact.** The cap tombstone @:29006 drops
   only the GENERAL channel; the CAP-EXEMPT channels (`xnu_live_usvc_*`, `xnu_live_stall_*`) publish
   to the very end of the log. Reading "no further syscalls" from the capped channel is exactly the
   [[mi4-measurement-defects]] class; the exempt channels show the syscalls CONTINUE.
5. The frozen stall frame (pc `0x8001b1fd`, sp `0xcfdbbf00`, lr `0x8046b92c`) is therefore a
   **HELD / STALE frame, not the live state** — the tick-driven sampler stopped updating while the
   syscall and paging paths kept going. This is consistent with a collapsed tick rate (below), and
   it is why the frame is a measurement artifact even though it is not (as earlier feared) a
   bit-garbled read: the read is fine, the frame is just old.

**The real frontier = the STALL, not the sleh return path.** The instrument's own summary says it:
`xnu_live_tick_sum_maxd` grows `0x3142d1 → 0x4b9fa4c → 0x1be8b709` — the MAXIMUM inter-tick gap
reaches **0x1be8b709 ≈ 24.4 s** while `tick_sum_count` (0x6b→0x1de) drags and `wdt_pets` climbs to
0x61. The machine keeps running (ticks, watchdog pets, storage) but tick DELIVERY collapses — the
same stall 999 measured ("rate collapse: 122 delivered = 98 fast + 24 slow") and 1003 named
("alive but no syscall progress ~23 min"). The 1003 STALL reading survives; the 1004 abort #40
reframe does not.

**What is NOT proven — the CAUSE of the stall.** The abort trace is complete and clean (a WALK);
the machine does not die at any abort; but between the last recorded events and the operator's
power press the tick cadence degrades to a 24.4 s gap. Two readings:
  (b1) **A lost/degraded timer interrupt** — the tick source is being delivered ever more slowly
       (an interrupt storm, a wedged GIC line, or the timer device itself); the samplers that ride
       the tick freeze while synchronous paths (syscalls, demand paging) continue. The frozen
       `enable_interrupts` frame is the LAST thing the tick saw.
  (b2) **A long-lived kernel critical section** — some path holds IRQs masked long enough that
       ticks queue and coalesce; the 24.4 s max gap is the coalescing.
  The tiebreaker (1005) is a TIMER-INDEPENDENT cadence witness: publish a monotonic counter bumped
  from a synchronous path (the USVC handler already exists) plus a per-tick counter, and read the
  RATIO at the end — if syscalls keep climbing while ticks fall, it is (b1); if both freeze
  together it is a full stop.

**What 1005 must NOT do:** it must not instrument `sleh_abort`'s return path (there is no evidence
of a hang there) and must not treat `sleh_back` as a death marker (the box is UNIPROCESSOR, so the
counter is not the claimed race either — the back-note's absence has NO proven cause, which is
exactly why it must not be read as one). The 1005 design still makes the two counters atomic as a
cheap MEASUREMENT hygiene fix, but the record is explicit that the fix removes a *potential*
false-positive, not an observed one.

**Why it matters:** dyld paged in ~24 of its own pages, touched the first shared-cache page (and
survived it), and then the SYSTEM's clock decayed until the run stopped making progress. The wall
is not the abort — it is what happens to the machine's timebase after dyld enters the shared
cache. Most of dyld's `ImageLoader::link` work still lies beyond it.

## 1005 input

The frontier is **the STALL** (tick delivery collapses to a 24.4 s max gap), NOT a hang in the
`sleh_abort` return path. Two instruments, both timer-independent:
1. **Cadence ratio witness** — publish a monotonic counter bumped from the USVC (syscall) path
   beside the per-tick counter, and read the RATIO at the end: syscalls climbing while ticks fall
   ⇒ (b1) degraded timer interrupt; both freezing together ⇒ a full stop. The USVC channel already
   exists (`xnu_live_usvc_*`) and already keeps publishing to the log's end.
2. **`sleh_seq`/`back` atomicity fix** — make the two counters `os_atomic` (or store the seq into
   the `_back` record itself) so a cross-CPU lost update cannot masquerade as a non-return. This is
   a MEASUREMENT fix ([[mi4-measurement-defects]]), not the frontier.
NOT for 1005: instrumenting `sleh_abort`'s return path (no evidence of a hang there) or treating
`sleh_back` as a death marker (`cpu_count=4`, non-atomic).
See `docs/1005-branch-decision-draft.md`; its branch (b) `STAGE90_XNU_FAULT_RATE` is still useful
to date faults, but the cadence-ratio witness is what answers the stall.
